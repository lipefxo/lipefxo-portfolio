# Four-house village — volumetric options

## Implemented A/C update

A and C now include a small 3 × 7 m social pool and rooftop solar on every house. They are implemented as switchable Three.js models at `/terrain`. Updated images: [A](../../public/terrain/concepts/a-promenade-pool-solar.png) and [C](../../public/terrain/concepts/c-garden-comb-pool-solar.png). Built-in Image Gen [edit prompts](pool-solar-prompts.md). Earlier studies below remain for reference.


Current direction: the entire approximately 20,003 m² lot; four elongated, detached, single-storey, three-bedroom pavilion homes in tijolo ecológico. Each home has its own kitchen, living space, bathrooms and laundry. Defined individual terraces and garden rooms connect to a shared paved public realm. No separate fenced residential plots.

## Latest concept images

- [A — The Promenade](a-promenade.png): four long-fronted pavilions in a single row, facing a continuous shared walk. The simplest, most legible village address. Rear access keeps most car movement away from the social frontage. Side planting defines each household's terrace. Recommended starting point for a shared daily dynamic.
- [B — Staggered Garden Lane](b-staggered-lane.png): homes alternate across a central walk, with offset terraces. More variety and separation; requires longer vehicle routes and carefully designed crossings. The entry court still needs four clearly marked visitor bays in the measured model.
- [C — The Garden Comb](c-garden-comb.png): parallel slender pavilions project away from the promenade. Their long sides form deeper individual garden rooms. Strongest household outdoor-space definition; short-end entrances mean verandas connect less directly to the collective walk. Keep hedges low and garden entrances open to avoid feeling like four fenced plots.

The social pavilion is for meetings, meals with friends and parties: a gathering hall, party-support kitchen/barbecue, toilets, storage and covered terrace. It is not shared everyday utilities or coworking. Position its event terrace away from bedroom gardens and test a planted separation zone; planting alone is not an acoustic solution.

## Common comparison program

| Element | Starting allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Compact house | 90–110 m² enclosed; image brief uses 6 × 17 m = 102 m² |
| Larger alternative retained | 150–180 m² enclosed; for example 7.5 × 22 m = 165 m² |
| Private veranda | Roughly 25–45 m² in addition to enclosed area |
| Social pavilion | Roughly 120–145 m² enclosed plus 50–80 m² terrace; adjust to party size |
| Resident parking | Two spaces beside each house, eight total |
| Shared visitor parking | Four spaces at entry; twelve total including resident spaces |
| Solar garden | Provisional 20 × 15 m reserve, not a sized energy system |
| Landscape | Moderate canopy target, approximately 25–35%, with several yellow and purple ipês |

Three bedrooms in a 90 m² house will require a compact program. Start nearer 105–110 m² for the independent kitchen/laundry requirement and verify with an actual floor plan. These are gross-area and massing targets, not validated room layouts. Larger homes can retain the same pavilion language by extending their length or modestly increasing width; preserve the common route and individual garden clearances when comparing sizes.

Use warm stone or concrete paving for the pedestrian spine and terraces, permeable interlocking paving for vehicle access and parking, defined planted beds and limited low garden walls. No dirt or gravel paths in the principal connections. Keep the solar reserve clear of mature tree shadows; orientation and capacity remain to be studied.

## What the images establish

Volumetric character, different site organizations, paving/planting language and the balance of individual and common space. Generated with the built-in Image Gen tool. Full prompts and the B correction prompt are in [prompts.md](prompts.md).

## What remains approximate

Image Gen does not preserve surveyed geometry, dimensions, parking counts or access widths reliably. The images are visual studies, not scaled site plans. The parcel remains assumed flat; true orientation, existing trees, drainage, setbacks, detailed vehicle turning and solar sizing have not been established. The exact outlines and programmed distances must be rebuilt in the metre-based Three.js model before treating them as spatially verified. The B correction restores its solar array to the inside of the site; visitor bay markings need reinstating. Some depicted trees, roof forms and parking arrangements are illustrative.

Tijolo ecológico is represented as warm exposed soil-cement masonry, consistent with [Sebrae's material description](https://bibliotecas.sebrae.com.br/chronus/ARQUIVOS_CHRONUS/IDEIAS_DE_NEGOCIO/PDFS/ideia-de-negocio_fabrica-de-tijolos-ecologicos.pdf). No structural design, cost or environmental performance is implied by the images.
